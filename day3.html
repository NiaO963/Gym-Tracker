      Rest and recovery

      • Calves and shins can feel tight after the hops and jump rope. If your shins ache, swap jumping for marching next time. Dull, achy soreness that fades over one to three days is normal. Sharp, pinching or joint pain is not. Stop that movement and get it checked in person.
      • Wrists can get sore from push-ups. Shake them out, and do the incline version if they complain.
      • Today was lighter on the joints, so an easy walk and gentle stretching are enough on your rest day.
      • Sleep 7 to 9 hours. Most muscle repair happens at night, and short sleep makes soreness and hunger worse.

      Eating to recover

      • Eat a balanced meal within a couple of hours: a palm or two of protein, a fist or two of carbohydrate, and vegetables.
      • Do not skip meals because the session was at home. Your muscles still need protein and energy to recover.
      • Spread protein across 3 to 4 meals. Research supports roughly 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight a day for building muscle. You do not need to share your weight; a palm or two of protein at each meal gets you close.
      • Drink water through the day and with your meal. Pale-yellow urine is a good sign that you are hydrated.

      General education, not medical or dietitian advice. If you have a health condition, a food allergy, take medication, or are pregnant or nursing, check with your doctor or a registered dietitian about eating and training.

      Stop any exercise that causes sharp or pinching pain. If something keeps hurting, see a physical therapist or doctor in person. Demo and muscle links open search results on YouTube and Google Images in a new tab.
